03 / POSITION

Toast is closest — and still leaves a gap

What Toast already owns

One cloud platform across POS, KDS, handhelds, table management, payments, payroll and team tools, xtraCHEF inventory, marketing, loyalty and AI. It is the closest thing to the Chefuffle thesis, and it is the benchmark buyers will name.

  • Strong breadth, but each module is a separate line item or quote.
  • Two-year contracts are standard; pricing rises with notice.
  • Close-out is reporting, not a server-by-server slip reconciliation workflow.
  • Voice ordering and AI are add-on surfaces, not the operating loop.

Where Chefuffle wins

The product is not “POS plus modules”. It is the whole shift in one real-time loop: table → course-aware KDS → check → split payment → close-out → reconciliation → next shift.

  • Course holds and fire-mains are native, not a KDS configuration project.
  • Server-level tallies, card-brand slip counts and cash due are built into End of Night.
  • Positions, permissions, onboarding and briefing are one system.
  • One price per location, month-to-month, with modules that switch on in-app.
The honest answer: there is no clean “all-in-one” in the market — Toast, Lightspeed, TouchBistro and SpotOn all sell a core plus modules. Chefuffle should compete on the operating loop, not on a longer feature list.

06 / RULES

Packaging rules that protect the model

Price per location, not per terminal. It removes the most common objection to Toast, TouchBistro and Clover, and makes adding a handheld a yes instead of a negotiation.
Keep inventory and reservations out of the base. They are the expansion revenue that turns a $79 account into $199–$349. Put them in Growth and Pro.
Charge per KDS screen. Every competitor does, and screens are real cost. Bundles include 2–3 screens; extras are $29.
Month-to-month is the weapon. Two- and three-year contracts are the market norm. Offer 15% off annual, never a lock-in.
Make the $0 path real. A free Core with 2.79% + 15¢ lets the smallest rooms start; at $35K volume the rate premium is roughly $105 / mo, so the economics still work.
Give every module a 14-day trial. Restaurants adopt what they can switch on for a shift. Activation, not a sales call, is the upgrade motion.
Charge for guided launch. Menu import, floor plan and staff setup are labor. $499 is inexpensive against a two-week launch, and it protects activation quality.
Voice AI carries usage. Minutes cost real money. $149 with 300 included, then $0.12/min, keeps margin without a surprise bill.
Budget check: restaurants spend roughly 2.6% of revenue on technology (3–5% by the common rule of thumb). A $1M restaurant can carry about $2,200–$4,100 / month across software, hardware and processing. Pro at $349 plus card fees and hardware sits comfortably inside that; the stack is priced for the middle, not the top.
